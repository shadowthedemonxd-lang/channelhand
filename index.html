Channelhand · early access

The helper behind AI‑run YouTube channels.

Say “upload this video” in chat. Channelhand puts it on your channel, fixes the title and tells you how it did — straight from your own computer.

See how it works Read the Privacy Policy

Four things, done well.

Channelhand is a small tool that an AI assistant can call on your behalf. It only touches the channels you sign in with, and only when you ask.

  1. 01

    Upload upload_video

    Sends a finished video from your computer to your channel with its title, description, tags, privacy and an optional thumbnail. Large files resume if the connection drops.

  2. 02

    Edit update_video

    Changes the title, description, tags, category or privacy of a video you already published. Anything you don't mention stays as it was.

  3. 03

    List list_my_videos

    Shows your latest uploads with their privacy, views, likes and comments, so the assistant knows what is already live.

  4. 04

    Measure get_analytics

    Reads your own channel's views, watch time and subscribers for any date range — overall, by day or by video.

How it works

Nothing runs on our servers, because there are none. Channelhand is a program on your own computer that your AI assistant talks to.

  1. Runs on your computer

    Channelhand is installed next to your AI assistant (for example Claude) as a local tool. Your videos never pass through anyone else's servers on the way to YouTube.

  2. You sign in with Google

    You connect each channel once through Google's own sign-in page. Channelhand never sees your password, and you can remove its access at any time.

  3. You ask, it acts

    Tell your assistant what you want in plain words. It calls Channelhand, which talks to YouTube API Services and reports back what happened.

Your data stays yours.
